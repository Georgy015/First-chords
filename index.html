The parts you'll hear about

Just enough vocabulary to follow along.

Tune up first

Standard tuning, low string to high. Tap a string to hear its note, then match your guitar to it by ear or with a tuner.

Six chords, click to learn

A chord is several notes played at the same time. On guitar, your fretting hand (i.e. your left hand if you're right-handed, and vice versa) holds down a few strings at set frets, then you strum, and the strings ring together as one fuller sound. Each chord has a name, like G or Am, and most songs are just a handful of chords played in order.

These cover a huge share of songs you already know. Tap a card to see how to place your fingers.

Numbers on the dots are fingers: 1 index, 2 middle, 3 ring, 4 pinky. ○ play the string open, ✕ don't play it.

Hints and tips

A few habits that make the first weeks easier.

Short sessions, often

Ten to fifteen minutes every day builds more than one long session a week.

Sore fingertips are normal

They toughen up in a week or two. Stop if anything hurts sharply.

Press just behind the fret

Put your fingertip right next to the metal fret, not in the middle of the gap. You need less pressure and get less buzzing.

Play on your fingertips

Keep your fingers arched so they don't touch and mute the strings next to them.

Check each string

Hold the chord and pick one string at a time. If one buzzes or sounds dead, adjust that finger.

Practise the changes

Switch back and forth between two chords, like Em to G, for a minute. Slow and clean beats fast and messy.

A basic strum pattern

Strumming is built in stages. Get steady with downstrokes first, then learn upstrokes on their own, then join them together. The full pattern is the goal at the end. Throughout, your strumming hand swings down and up like a pendulum, in time with the count, even on the slots where it skips the strings.
