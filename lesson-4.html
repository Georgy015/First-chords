Lesson 4 · Warm-up

Stretch those fingers!

Barre chords are coming next, and they ask more of your fretting hand than anything so far: a strong index finger and four fingers that move on their own. These two warm-ups build exactly that. Play them for a few minutes at the start of every practice session.

4.1

Before you start

  • One finger per fretEach finger owns one fret: index on the first, pinky on the fourth. Your hand stays put; only the fingers move.
  • Slow and evenEvery note gets the same length and the same volume. Clean at 60 BPM beats messy at 100.
  • Stay relaxedThumb behind the neck, wrist loose. If your hand aches, stop and shake it out. A slight burn in the fingertips is fine; pain is not.

Moving across the neck

Both exercises use the same finger order at any fret. Pick a starting fret to move the whole pattern up the neck. Higher up, the frets are closer together, so it's easier on a short stretch; down at fret 1 it's the biggest stretch. Tick Climb the neck to move up one fret after every round, all the way to fret 9.

60 BPM

4.2

Exercise 1: The Spider

Four notes on every string, one finger after the other, from the low E up to high e and back down. Leave each finger down until the next one has landed, then lift it just off the string.

4.3

Exercise 2: The Crossover

The same four frets in a mixed order: 1, 3, 2, 4. Your middle and ring fingers have to work independently, which is the hardest part of a barre chord shape. Expect it to feel clumsy at first.

Coming up: barre chords

Your first barre chord

In a barre chord, your index finger presses all six strings at once, like a movable nut, while the other three fingers make a chord shape. That's why these warm-ups train the index finger and finger independence. Next lesson starts gently, with an easy "mini F", before the full F and Bm.
