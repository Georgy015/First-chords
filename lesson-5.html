Lesson 5 · Barre chords

Barre chords: the key to the whole neck

So far, every chord you've played has leaned on the open strings at the top of the neck. Barre chords take that crutch away: your index finger lays flat across the strings and becomes a movable nut. It's the step where the guitar stops being a handful of shapes and starts being an instrument you can find your way around.

5.1

Why barre chords are worth it

A barre chord is a chord shape you can slide anywhere. Once your hand knows it, a lot opens up at once.

  • 1

    Every chord, anywhere

    One shape, moved up and down the neck, gives you every major chord. A second shape gives you every minor chord. No more songs you can't play because of an F or a B♭.

  • 2

    More songs, more subtle sound

    Bm, F♯m, F and their cousins show up in a huge number of songs. Barre chords also let you play the same chord higher up the neck, for a fuller or sweeter version of it.

  • 3

    New rhythms

    Ease off the pressure for a split second and every string goes quiet. That one trick gives you choppy, muted strums: the backbone of funk, reggae, ska and a lot of pop.

  • 4

    Melody and improvising

    Barre shapes are signposts on the neck. Scales, riffs and solos sit right around them, so they're your way into playing melodies and improvising over chords.

What it looks like in your hand

An F barre chord, seen from the front, the way someone facing you would see it: headstock to the right, low E string at the top. Your thumb is hidden behind the neck.

  • 1Index: flat across all six strings, just behind fret 1. Roll it slightly onto its bony side; it presses harder there.
  • 2Middle: G string, fret 2.
  • 3Ring: A string, fret 3.
  • 4Pinky: D string, fret 3, right next to the ring finger.
  • TThumb: hidden behind the neck, roughly behind your middle finger. Squeeze between thumb and index, not with the whole arm.
“Ouch.”

Yes, they feel hard at first. That's normal.

Almost every guitarist remembers their first barre chords: strings that buzz, a tired index finger, a sound that's more thud than chord. That isn't a sign you're doing it wrong. Your hand simply hasn't built the strength and the feel for it yet.

It's the beginning, not the whole story. What it asks for is regular, short practice: five to ten minutes a day, starting with the warm-ups from Lesson 4. Every guitarist who plays barre chords today went through exactly this, and the payoff is far bigger than the struggle.

  • Week 1Some strings buzz or sound dead. Your index finger gets tired fast.
  • Weeks 2–3Most strings ring. Changing into the chord is still slow.
  • Week 4 and onThe shape lands cleanly, and you start using it in songs.

5.2

The mini F

Before you barre six strings, barre two. The mini F uses only the top four strings, and your index finger presses just the B and high e strings at once. It's a real F chord, it sounds good in songs, and it teaches your index finger the one skill every barre chord needs: pressing more than one string evenly.

F (mini)
  1. Index (1): lay it flat across the B and high e strings at fret 1. Let the fingertip stick out a little past the high e.
  2. Middle (2): G string, fret 2, on its tip.
  3. Ring (3): D string, fret 3, on its tip.
  4. Strum only the four thinnest strings, starting from the D. Skip the low E and A.

String check

Hold the shape and pick one string at a time. Tap each string below to hear how it should sound, compare it with your guitar, and tick it when yours rings just as clearly.

If a string buzzes or sounds dead

  • B or high e is dull: roll your index finger slightly onto its bony, thumb-facing side and move it closer to the fret wire.
  • G string is muted: your index finger is leaning on it. Arch the middle finger more so the index lies flat only on the top two strings.
  • D string buzzes: press the ring finger on its very tip, right behind fret 3.
  • Hand gets tired: squeeze less. Press just hard enough for a clean note, then relax between strums.

Too hard today? Play Fmaj7 instead: the same shape with your index on the B string only, high e open. It sounds softer and dreamier, and it's a perfect stepping stone.

Practice task: C ↔ F

C to F is one of the most common chord changes there is. Good news: your index finger stays on the B string at fret 1 and simply flattens to cover high e as well. Middle and ring keep their shape and both move one string over: middle from D to G, ring from A to D. Use Stage 1 (downstrokes) until it's clean at 70 BPM, then Stage 2.
